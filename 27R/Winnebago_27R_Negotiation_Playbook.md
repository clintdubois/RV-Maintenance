## Leverage to use

- **Multi-location competition**: Poulsbo RV has this same 27R listed at Sumner, Kent, and Mt. Vernon. Get a written out-the-door quote from each and let them bid against each other.
- **Outside competing quote**: Get a quote from a high-volume out-of-state dealer (e.g., Lichtsinn RV, Motor Home Specialist) as leverage, even if freight would make buying there impractical.
- **Cash, no-contingency offer**: This is a cash purchase — no trade-in, no financing contingency. State that up front. It removes the dealer's usual excuses for holding price firm and lets you negotiate a single, clean out-the-door number.
- **Trade-in stays off the table**: There isn't one, so there's nothing to bundle margin into — keep the entire conversation on the unit price itself.

## Before comparing "% off" between units

Get the window sticker / build sheet for the *specific VIN* being quoted. MSRP moves with options (solar, awnings, upgraded suspension, etc.), so two 27R units at different price points may not be an apples-to-apples discount comparison. Ask Poulsbo for:
- The factory MSRP breakdown by option/package for the exact unit
- Confirmation of what's dealer-added vs. factory-installed (dealer add-ons like paint protection, extended warranties are usually the most negotiable/removable line items)

## Negotiation script / sequence

1. Request written out-the-door quotes (price + tax/fees/doc charges) from all three Poulsbo locations for their specific in-stock units, stating up front that this is a cash purchase.
2. Get one outside comparison quote for leverage.
3. Counter with a target in the 25–30%-off-MSRP range, framed as "I have quotes near this from elsewhere."
4. Negotiate price first and in writing — a single cash out-the-door number, nothing bundled in.
5. Once price is settled, review the line-item fee sheet for anything padded (doc fees, add-on packages) before signing.

## Extended warranty negotiation

**Your prior result:** negotiated an **84-month/50,000-mile** extended warranty from an $11,000 opening quote down to **$4,100** (~63% off) on a previous purchase. That's consistent with what forums report as normal — treat it as the benchmark to aim for again, not an outlier.

### Why the F&I number starts so high

- Dealer finance & insurance (F&I) departments quote extended warranties/service contracts at **150–300%+ markup** over their wholesale cost.
- Reported wholesale cost for these contracts is typically **$1,500–$3,500**; dealers routinely quote **$4,000–$6,000+** at the counter, and can go higher (into five figures) on complex/diesel chassis units like a Sprinter-based View, where "bumper-to-bumper" coverage includes both the coach and the Mercedes chassis/drivetrain.
- An $11k opening ask on a diesel Sprinter Class C is plausible precisely because of that chassis complexity — but the actual cost basis underneath it doesn't scale nearly that much versus a gas chassis unit.
- Reported real-world examples on iRV2: $3,300 for a 7-year policy, $5,000 for a 4-year policy, ~$3,400 for a 5-year diesel policy, one "diesel wrap" quoted just under $10k before negotiation. One buyer got a dealer down 40% just by pushing back.

### Tactics (same ones that got you to $4,100 last time)

1. **Negotiate the warranty *after* the unit price is locked, but *before* signing anything.** Keep it as a separate line item — don't let it get bundled into the "monthly payment" framing, which hides the markup.
2. **Treat the first number as an opening offer**, exactly like the RV price itself. There is enormous margin to give.
3. **Get an outside quote for comparison** before you sit down with F&I — third-party administrators (Wholesale Warranties, XtraRide direct, Route 66 via a credit union, Compass) sell the same underlying contracts without showroom overhead, often 50%+ cheaper. Even just naming a lower quote number moves the dealer's price.
4. **No urgency — you don't have to decide that day.** Extended service contracts can be purchased weeks or months after delivery (as long as the unit is still within age/mileage eligibility), direct from a broker. Reminding F&I of this removes their main pressure lever.
5. **Ask what's actually excluded.** Diesel/Sprinter chassis contracts vary a lot in whether they cover chassis electronics, turbo, emissions systems, etc. — a cheaper contract with better exclusion terms can beat an expensive one with more gaps.
